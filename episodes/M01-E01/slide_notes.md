# Slide notes — M01-E01

## Working title

What is a material? A biologist's translation

## Slide sequence

### 1. Cold open — A biological tissue under force

**Visual:** macro or microscopy shot of tendon, leaf, orchid stigma, pollinarium, silk, or wood.  
**Message:** This is not only biology. It is material behaviour.

Narration idea:

> You already know structure and function. Materials science gives you a physical language for that relationship.

### 3. The wrong definition

**Visual:** simple list: steel, glass, plastic, rubber, wood, bone, silk, hydrogel.

**Message:** A material is not merely a named substance.

---

### 4. A better definition

**Core statement:**

A material is matter organised across scales so that it performs under physical constraints.

**Visual:** hierarchy ladder from macro to molecular scale.

---

### 5. Why biologists already think this way

**Visual:** biological structure-function examples.

Examples:

- bone resists fracture while remaining light
- leaf cuticle controls water loss
- spider silk stores and dissipates energy
- orchid pollinia transfer reproductive tissue as a mechanical package

---

### 6. The materials framework

**Visual:** processing → structure → properties → performance loop.

**Key translation:**

- Processing = growth, secretion, mineralisation, self-assembly, synthesis, manufacturing
- Structure = hierarchy, morphology, microstructure, molecular arrangement
- Properties = stiffness, toughness, viscosity, adhesion, permeability
- Performance = survival, movement, protection, reproduction, clinical/engineering use

---

### 7. Substance vs structure

**Visual:** same chemical base, different structures.

Examples:

- cellulose in cotton vs wood vs bacterial cellulose
- collagen in tendon vs skin vs bone
- chitin in fungal walls vs insect cuticle

**Message:** Composition matters, but structure often decides performance.

---

### 8. Properties are measured, not guessed

**Visual:** simple stress-strain curve and examples of mechanical tests.

Terms introduced:

- stiffness
- strength
- toughness
- hardness
- viscosity
- adhesion

Do not go deep yet. This episode only introduces the idea that function can be quantified.

---

### 9. Biological function as material performance

**Visual:** matrix table: biological function vs material performance.

| Biological function | Material interpretation |
|---|---|
| protection | impact resistance, hardness, toughness |
| movement | elasticity, actuation, damping |
| attachment | adhesion, friction, interfacial mechanics |
| transport | permeability, porosity, capillarity |
| signalling | optical, acoustic, chemical response |
| reproduction | adhesion, packaging, fracture, release |
